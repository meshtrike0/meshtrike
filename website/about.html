ABOUT

Networks get cut. People nearby can still talk.

Meshtrike exists because the moments you most need to reach someone are often the moments the network fails: when a crowd overloads the tower, when a storm takes it down, or when someone switches it off on purpose.

01 · WHY

The phone in your pocket is already a radio.

Most messengers send every word through a company’s servers, over a mobile network that someone else controls. That works until it doesn’t. When it fails, two people standing a few metres apart can’t send each other a single line of text, even though both their phones have Bluetooth and Wi-Fi radios that could talk directly.

Meshtrike uses those radios. Phones talk to each other directly and carry sealed messages for one another, so a message can hop across a crowd, a town or a valley without a tower, a server or an account. Nobody in between can read it or tell who it’s for.

We build it for festivals and stadiums, for protests and shutdowns, for storms and earthquakes, and for trails and boats far from any signal. And we build it to be honest about its limits, because people in hard places deserve to know exactly what they can rely on.

02 · PRINCIPLES

What we won’t compromise on.

  • Open source

    Everything is public under Apache-2.0: the Rust core, the Android app, MeshGRID and this website.

  • No accounts

    No phone number, no email, no sign-up. Your identity is a key made on your phone.

  • No servers required

    There is no Meshtrike server. Future community servers will help, but none will ever be required.

  • The app never touches the internet

    Meshtrike doesn’t hold Android’s internet permission. The optional MeshGRID add-on carries only sealed messages.

  • No telemetry

    No analytics, no crash reporting, nothing sent home. The build checks for it on every change.

  • Honest about limits

    We say what Meshtrike can’t protect you from, and that it hasn’t been independently audited yet.

03 · WHO

Made by the Meshtrike team.

We’re a small, pseudonymous team. That’s deliberate: the people Meshtrike is for often can’t afford to be named, and the people who build it shouldn’t have to be either. Don’t take our word for anything. Judge us by the code, the whitepaper and how we handle problems.
